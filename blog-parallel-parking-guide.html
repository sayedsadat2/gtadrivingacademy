Parallel Parking Made Simple: Step-by-Step Guide for Your G2

Oct 14, 2024
GTA Driving Academy Team
4 min read
5,532 views

Ask any G2 candidate what maneuver worries them most, and parallel parking usually tops the list. The good news: it's a completely mechanical skill. Once you understand the reference points and steps, it becomes muscle memory — no guesswork required.

Why Parallel Parking Feels So Hard

Parallel parking feels hard because it's the only common maneuver where you're steering while moving backward and judging distance you can't directly see. Most of the anxiety disappears once you stop trying to "eyeball it" and instead learn fixed reference points on your own car.

Step 1: Positioning Your Car

Pull up alongside the car in front of the space you're parking in, leaving about a metre of space between vehicles. Your rear bumper should be roughly aligned with theirs. This starting position is what makes every step after it predictable.

What to do: Practice this alignment step alone first — in an empty lot, pull up next to a cone or parked car and check your position in the mirror before moving on. Getting step 1 right makes everything else fall into place.

"Parallel parking isn't about talent — it's about knowing where to look and when to turn the wheel. Everyone can learn it in a few sessions." — GTA Driving Academy instructor team

Step 2: The Reverse Turn

With your car positioned, turn to check your mirrors and blind spot, then begin reversing slowly. When your rear seat (roughly) lines up with the rear bumper of the car beside you, turn the wheel fully toward the curb and continue reversing slowly.

What to do: Go slowly enough that you can stop the instant something looks off. Speed is never the goal in parallel parking — control is.

Step 3: Straightening Out

As your car angles into the space, watch your front bumper clear the rear bumper of the car ahead, then straighten your wheel and continue reversing until your car is roughly parallel with the curb.

What to do: Check both mirrors continuously through this step — you're watching two reference points at once (front clearance and rear position), which is exactly why slow, controlled movement matters.

Step 4: Final Position Check

Pull forward slightly if needed to centre yourself in the space. On your G2 test, examiners are checking that you're within about 30cm of the curb and reasonably centred between the two vehicles — not touching either one.

What to do: Once parked, glance at your position relative to the curb through your side window. If you're too far out or too close, a small forward-and-back adjustment is completely acceptable — and expected.

How to Practice Without a Test Car

  • Use cones or empty water bottles in an empty parking lot to simulate two parked cars
  • Practice the same spot repeatedly until the steps feel automatic, then vary the space size
  • Have someone watch from outside the car the first few times to give you real-time feedback on distance
  • Don't skip straight to a tight space — start wide and shrink the gap as you improve
