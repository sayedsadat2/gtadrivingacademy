10 Things Your Driving Instructor Wishes You Knew Before Lesson 1

Jun 15, 2024
GTA Driving Academy Team
8 min read
7,215 views

After years of first lessons with hundreds of students, we've noticed the same worries and misconceptions come up again and again. If you've got a first lesson coming up, here's what we wish every student walked in already knowing.

1. It's Completely Normal to Be Nervous

Nearly every student arrives at their first lesson nervous, whether they show it or not. You are not behind, behind schedule, or unusual for feeling anxious about it. It's a genuinely new and unfamiliar skill, and treating the nerves as normal — rather than a sign something's wrong — makes the whole experience easier.

2. Small Steering Inputs Go a Long Way

New drivers almost universally over-steer at first, turning the wheel far more than the car actually needs to change direction. A car responds to small, smooth inputs — think of steering as guiding, not wrestling.

What to expect: Your instructor will likely say "less wheel" more than anything else in your first couple of lessons. That's completely normal and corrects quickly with practice.

3. Check Your Mirrors Before You Move, Not After

One of the most common early habits to build is checking mirrors and blind spots before any movement — not as an afterthought. This becomes automatic surprisingly fast, but it has to be consciously practiced at first.

"The habits you build in your first five lessons are the ones that stick for life. That's why we're so particular about the small stuff early on." — GTA Driving Academy instructor team

4. Your Instructor Has a Brake Too

Every GTA Driving Academy vehicle has a dual-brake setup, meaning your instructor can stop the car independently if needed. This isn't something we use often, but knowing it's there tends to genuinely calm first-lesson nerves.

5. There's No Such Thing as a Bad Question

Students often stay quiet about confusion out of embarrassment, then repeat the same mistake because they didn't want to "ask something dumb." Every instructor would always rather answer a question than watch the same error repeat.

6. Wear Comfortable Shoes

This sounds minor, but it isn't. Thick-soled shoes, heels, or flip-flops all reduce your ability to feel pedal pressure accurately — something that matters a lot for smooth braking and gas control, especially early on.

7. Progress Isn't a Straight Line

A great fourth lesson followed by a rough fifth lesson is completely normal and doesn't mean you're regressing. Skill-building naturally has some unevenness to it — what matters is the overall trend, not any single lesson.

8. Practice Between Lessons If You Can

Students who get supervised practice time with a licensed adult between lessons (even just around a quiet neighbourhood) consistently progress faster than those who only drive during lessons. Practice reinforces what was just taught while it's fresh.

9. Mistakes Are Expected — That's the Point of Lessons

If you never made a mistake, you wouldn't need lessons. Stalling, overcorrecting, missing a shoulder check — these are exactly what lessons exist to work through, in a controlled setting with an instructor who can guide you through it safely.

10. Driving Is a Skill, Not a Talent

Some students assume driving comes naturally to certain people and not others. In our experience, it's almost entirely a learnable skill — built through repetition, feedback, and time behind the wheel, not innate talent. Anyone who commits to the process improves.
