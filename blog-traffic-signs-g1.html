20 Traffic Signs Every Ontario G1 Student Must Know

May 28, 2024
Fahim Yarzai
4 min read
8,340 views

Road sign questions make up a large portion of the Ontario G1 knowledge test, and they're consistently where students lose the most marks — not because the signs are hard, but because most people have never studied them systematically. Here are the 20 signs we see missed most often in practice tests, grouped by category the way the MTO handbook organizes them.

Regulatory Signs (White and Red/Black)

These signs tell you what you must or must not do — they're not suggestions. Missing these on the test, or in real driving, usually means missing a legal requirement.

  • No Standing vs. No Parking vs. No Stopping — three visually similar red-and-white signs with three different meanings and different penalty levels
  • Do Not Enter — a solid red circle with a white bar, marking one-way restrictions
  • Yield — the inverted red-and-white triangle, distinct from Stop's octagon
  • Keep Right — a blue circle with a white arrow, marking a mandatory direction around an obstruction
  • School Zone speed signs — fluorescent yellow-green, marking reduced speed periods

Warning Signs (Yellow, Diamond-Shaped)

These alert you to hazards or changing road conditions ahead — they describe what's coming, not a rule.

  • Pedestrian Crossing vs. School Crossing — similar silhouettes, different contexts and required caution levels
  • Merging Traffic vs. Lane Ends — both diamond warnings but requiring different responses
  • Slippery When Wet — a car with wavy skid lines, commonly confused with other road-condition signs
  • Winding Road — the S-curve symbol, often paired with an advisory speed sign below it
  • Deer Crossing — common on GTA-adjacent rural roads, frequently missed by city-based students
"Students memorize shapes but forget that colour carries meaning too — yellow means caution, red means a legal requirement, green means direction. That distinction alone answers half the sign questions." — Fahim Yarzai, Instructor

Information and Guide Signs (Green and Blue)

These don't involve rules at all — they're purely informational, and the test still checks whether you can quickly read them under time pressure.

  • Green highway direction signs — testing your ability to read exit numbers and distances correctly
  • Blue service signs (gas, hospital, food) — rarely tested directly but appear as distractor answers
  • HOV lane signs — diamond symbol on a green or white sign, increasingly common on GTA highways

Construction and Temporary Signs (Orange)

Orange signs mean temporary conditions — construction zones, detours, flag persons — and Ontario law requires reduced speed and extra caution in these zones, with doubled fines in active construction areas.

  • Reduced Speed Ahead in construction zones — often paired with a specific posted speed
  • Flag Person Ahead — requires full stop readiness, not just caution
  • Lane Closed Ahead — merging arrow indicating which lane to move into and when

The Fastest Way to Actually Memorize These

Reading a list once (like this one) helps, but real retention comes from active recall. Cover the sign name and quiz yourself on meaning from the shape and colour alone, then flip it — describe a scenario and recall which sign applies.

What to do: Use flashcards or a sign-quiz app for 10 minutes a day in the week before your test, rather than one long cram session. Spaced repetition beats a single marathon study session for this kind of memorization.
